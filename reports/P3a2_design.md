The retained artifact `artifacts/p3a/p3a-gate/harness-T2-1001046.json.gz` and all referenced exception parts are the evidence. The harness solver attempted to overwrite `/opt/qi/harness.py`; the read-only boundary denied it and the audited command exited 1, recording PolicyFailure and policy evidence. E2Harness nevertheless transferred empty stdout to `/work/submission.json`. That trusted Docker exec transfer exceeded the 60-second host deadline: CancelledError inside asyncio.wait_for became TimeoutError, then HostTimeout, then the framework HarnessError, finally run_policy's RuntimeError. The attempt lasted 62.90606819203822 seconds, cleaned its container, and did not retry. Policy-first classification mislabeled the terminating host operation as policy. The gate stopped scheduling on that terminal row. The daemon stall's underlying cause is unknown: empty stdin is handled explicitly by the existing transport and is not established as the cause.

## Changes fixed before implementation

1. Recorded solver policy evidence closes E2 as a complete hard-zero outcome. Once flagged, harness launch skips submission transfer and finalize skips source/submission reads; scoring skips all patch execution and replay. Preserve audited commands, container provenance and policy evidence. Policy status is monotonic. Optional post-policy evidence collection cannot convert that outcome into an incomplete scientific row. Cleanup remains mandatory and its errors remain visible; failed cleanup fails infrastructure criteria even when the scientific reward is already zero.
2. Exception classification follows the terminating operation, independent of prior policy evidence. Typed host timeout/daemon failures in the active cause chain are infrastructure; unrelated earlier policy exceptions cannot dominate them. Preserve full chains, including framework errors that discard causes via an attempt-local terminal-operation record. Infrastructure failures get the existing at-most-one whole-rollout retry with a new container after complete cleanup. Carry any previous policy evidence forward as a hard-zero reward obligation. A completed policy outcome stays complete even if later infrastructure/cleanup fails, while infrastructure health and cleanup failures independently fail the gate. Non-policy terminal infrastructure after retry is incomplete. Do not reinterpret cancellation as a host timeout without typed evidence.
3. Schedule every planned row even after terminal failures or retry exhaustion. Keep every attempt. The shared retry budget remains floor(planned/200); exhausted rows retain retry-limit failures while other initial attempts continue. Gate fails for any incomplete row, any infrastructure/cleanup failure unresolved by its permitted retry, or retries above 0.5%. Source-integrity/storage preconditions remain fatal safety guards rather than permission to silently omit rows.
4. Record each trusted transfer's operation, byte counts, monotonic duration, success/failure, typed exception and a separately timed minimal Docker daemon probe. Do not log credentials or command payloads. Probe failure cannot mask the transfer's original exception. Bound telemetry and daemon probes. Start and retain the 60-second transport deadline unless stress telemetry provides a documented reason to change it before freezing. Fresh gate concurrency is 8.
